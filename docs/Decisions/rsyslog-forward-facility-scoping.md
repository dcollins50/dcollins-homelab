Scoped down the `*.* @10.0.10.10:5144` rsyslog forwarder on pve-env1, pve-services, and pve-env2, which was shipping every facility at every priority to [[soc-stack-vm]] over UDP with no filtering.

## Decision
Changed line 70 of `/etc/rsyslog.conf` on all three hosts from:

```
*.* @10.0.10.10:5144
```

to:

```
*.*;kern.none;daemon.none    @10.0.10.10:5144
```

Keeps auth, authpriv, cron, mail, user, and local0-7 facilities flowing to the `proxmox-logs` index. Drops `kern` and `daemon` entirely from this forward.

## Reasoning
Investigated a reported "UDP flood on SSH login" from pve-env1 to soc-stack:5144. Captured 90 packets with `tcpdump` during a single SSH login and read the payloads. Two findings:

1. The line was `*.* @<dest>`, meaning literally every log message on the box gets forwarded, not just security-relevant ones. An SSH login just happens to generate a burst (sshd, pam_unix, systemd-logind) that makes the volume spike noticeably at that moment. It isn't an SSH-specific problem, it's constant background noise that happens to spike then.
2. Half the captured packets were kernel AppArmor audit denials generated by the diagnostic `tcpdump` command itself (`kern` facility), and one was filebeat's own internal 30-second monitoring heartbeat (`daemon` facility, PRI 30 decodes to daemon.info). Neither is meaningful security or operational signal.

Chose to exclude `kern` and `daemon` specifically (evidence-driven) rather than either:
- Narrowing to `auth,authpriv.*` only: would have killed general host visibility that the `proxmox-logs` index and its Kibana dashboard were built for (cron failures, disk/service issues, package changes), not just what showed up in this one capture.
- Widening to `auth,authpriv,daemon,syslog.*`: would keep `daemon`, which is exactly the facility the filebeat noise came through, so it wouldn't have fixed anything found.

Trade-off accepted: this also drops legitimate kernel warnings (disk errors, OOM killer) and legitimate daemon-level errors from this forward. Acceptable because Wazuh agents are already deployed on all three hosts and read local logs directly (including kernel and daemon logs), independent of this rsyslog forward, so no detection capability is actually lost, just no longer double-shipped raw to a second pipeline.

## What was ruled out
- Treating this as an SSH-specific issue and looking for a PAM/SSH-side cause. The `*.*` line explains the observed behavior fully; there is no separate SSH-triggered mechanism.
- pve-gateway was checked and excluded from this fix: its line 70 uses `@@10.0.10.10:5144` (double `@`, TCP), not the single-`@` UDP form the other three hosts use. Different transport, not part of this problem. Left as-is; the TCP vs UDP inconsistency across nodes is a separate open item, not yet resolved.

## Verification
Applied via a small script looping `ssh -t` to each host (backup config, `sed` the line, validate with `rsyslogd -N1` before restarting, restart, confirm `active`). Confirmed the updated line on pve-env1, pve-services, and pve-env2 via grep, and confirmed `rsyslog.service` reported `active` on all three after restart.
